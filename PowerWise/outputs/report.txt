1. Cut always-on (stand-by) load  (about Rs.2,290 per year)
    Between 1 am and 5 am you still use about 0.25 kWh every hour (fridge, router, TV/set-top box, chargers). That is 2,181 kWh a year (38% of your total). Switching off at the plug and replacing an old fridge could trim about 15% of it (327 kWh).
2. Set the AC 2 degC higher  (about Rs.393 per year)
    Your usage rises by about 1.06 kWh per day for every degC above 25 degC. Cooling-related use is roughly 467 kWh a year. As a rule of thumb each 1 degC higher AC setting saves about 6%; also use ceiling fans and keep filters clean.
3. Shift some usage out of 18:00-21:59  (about Rs.1,167 per year)
    If your utility charges more in peak hours (assumed 1.3x the normal rate), moving 15% of peak-window use (washing machine, dishwasher, water pump, EV/phone charging) to off-peak hours saves about Rs.1,167 a year. Even without such a tariff it lowers peak load on the grid.
4. Look at weekend usage  (about Rs.143 per year)
    Weekends use 2.0 kWh/day more than weekdays (17.0 vs 15.1). Run full loads only, avoid leaving lights, fans and TVs on in empty rooms, and spread laundry across the week.
